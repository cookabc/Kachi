Deal season has reshaped the sector. The proposed merger of two mid-cap producers would create the third-largest independent, and the acquisition follows a year in which buyers paid premium multiples for scale. Analysts expect further consolidation as smaller players struggle with opex and working capital.
The company completed the divestiture of its pipeline unit for $2.3 billion, a spin-off that leaves the parent focused on upstream assets. Management said the carve-out would simplify the group structure.
Third-quarter results included a $780 million impairment charge tied to a gas field whose reserves were revised lower, and a smaller write-down on inventory. Excluding those items, gross profit rose 6% and net income was flat.
The restructuring programme will cut $400 million of annual overhead, with a one-off cost of $150 million. Capital expenditure guidance was trimmed to $9 billion, while operating expenditure per barrel fell 8%.
Free cash flow of $4.1 billion covered the dividend and reduced net debt to $12 billion. Return on equity improved to 14%, and the payout ratio stayed at 45% of earnings per share.
Book value per share rose to $38, leaving the stock trading near one times book value — a p/b multiple well below the five-year average. On a p/e basis the shares look cheaper than peers, though the discount reflects lower guidance for the year ahead.
Revenue beat estimates at $21.4 billion, but earnings per share missed consensus by three cents on higher headwinds from freight and a tailwind that failed to materialise in Asia. The outlook for the fourth quarter assumes flat pricing.
The prospectus for the listing was published this week, with shares expected to price at a discount to the last private round. Proceeds will fund the acquisition pipeline and repay a bridge loan.
Gross margin expanded 180 basis points to 34%, and operating margin reached 19%. Management characterised the result as a beat on both revenue and earnings, with a modest miss on volume.
Share issuance funded part of the deal; the buyback programme was paused for two quarters while net debt was reduced. The quarterly payout was maintained at 22 cents.

Capex discipline remains the theme: several producers guided capital spending lower, and management said capex would stay within the previous range even if prices improve.
Leverage on the balance sheet fell to 1.2 times net debt to EBITDA, the lowest in three years, giving room for a further acquisition without a rights issue.
Tailwinds from lower freight costs and a weaker currency offset some of the pricing pressure; without those tailwinds the miss would have been wider.

A sharp fall in collateral values can trigger margin calls, forcing holders to post cash or sell assets into a falling market.
The board framed the restructuring as protecting value for all stakeholders, not only shareholders — employees, suppliers and lenders included.
