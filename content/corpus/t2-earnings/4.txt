Archer-Daniels-Midland lifted its 2026 profit outlook on Tuesday after beating Wall Street's second-quarter expectations, supported by strong crop processing margins and favorable U.S. biofuels policies.
Soaring energy prices linked to the Iran war boosted margins for corn-based ethanol production and for crushing soybeans into renewable diesel feedstocks. Higher U.S. biofuel blending mandates also removed uncertainty that had been weighing on earnings.
The improved margins signal a turnaround for ADM and rivals after a global grain glut and trade disruptions hurt agribusiness profits in recent quarters. A rival raised its own 2026 earnings forecast last week, citing strong margins and a solid demand outlook.
All three business segments posted sharply higher operating profits in the quarter, including its nutrition unit, which had struggled to hit earnings targets in recent years.
Management said the results showed a marked increase in earnings, helped by a constructive biofuels environment, strong commercial and operational execution, and growing momentum in nutrition.
The company raised its adjusted 2026 earnings forecast to $5.15 to $5.60 per share, up from a previous range of $4.15 to $4.70, citing improvements in its crushing and ethanol businesses and expectations for continued supportive margins.
Crop processing volumes climbed as grain prices rallied after the start of the war, prompting farmers to sell corn and soybeans they had kept in storage during a prolonged period of low prices.
Operating profit in the ag services and oilseeds unit, its largest segment, jumped 129% from a year earlier. Gross margins expanded after the government ordered refiners to blend record amounts of biofuels into gasoline and diesel this year and next.
The company said it has identified 10 soybean processing plants for possible expansion, part of a capital expenditure programme that will lift capex above the prior year.
Strong grain export demand also supported the merchant. Management said the largest importer appeared on track to meet its commitment to buy 25 million metric tons this year.
For the three months ended June 30, the company posted adjusted earnings of $1.84 per share, above the average analyst estimate of $1.44 — a clear beat on both earnings and revenue.
Free cash flow covered the dividend, and net debt fell as working capital normalised. Equity rose on retained earnings, and leverage measured as net debt to EBITDA improved.
