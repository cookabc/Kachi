Chevron exceeded Wall Street expectations for second-quarter earnings, posting its strongest quarterly profit in at least six years as the Iran conflict roils global energy markets and lifts results for the industry's largest players.
The company reported adjusted earnings of $12 billion, or $6.06 per share, topping the $5.56 average analyst forecast compiled by LSEG. Shares advanced 2% to close at $196.83.
The performance aligned with European energy giants TotalEnergies and Shell, which also delivered blockbuster quarterly results thanks to elevated crude prices. ExxonMobil, meanwhile, fell short of profit projections despite hitting a four-year earnings high.
Amid all the geopolitical uncertainty and market volatility that's still upon us, we continue to deliver the reliable energy that the world has needed, the chief financial officer told Reuters.
The second-largest U.S. oil producer carries less Middle East output than its competitors, enabling it to capture the upside from higher prices while avoiding the significant supply disruptions that affected rivals including Exxon and TotalEnergies.
The chief executive cautioned that the ongoing hostilities would keep straining global supply chains. Every day that goes by, the situation gets more difficult, he told CNBC.
Upstream earnings reached $8.2 billion, a 200% jump from a year earlier. Brent crude averaged 23% higher in the second quarter compared with the first three months of the year, with shipments through the Strait of Hormuz constrained.
Total production rose to 4 million barrels of oil equivalent per day in the quarter, up from 3.85 million in the first. U.S. output set a new record at 2.08 million boepd.
Chevron said it expects per-barrel spending on U.S. shale production to fall 25% this year compared with 2025, citing operational efficiencies.
Downstream profits also climbed to their highest level since 2020 at $4.9 billion, supported by record U.S. refinery throughput. Tight global fuel inventories and Middle East tensions pushed refining margins to unprecedented highs.
An analyst attributed the earnings beat largely to stronger-than-expected downstream performance, noting the results reflected solid operational execution and steady strategy.
The outsized profits may attract renewed criticism from the president, who last month accused oil companies of price gouging and pressed them to do more to reduce gasoline costs.
Chevron bought back $3 billion in stock during the quarter and paid $3.5 billion in dividends, matching the prior quarter's outlay.
The chief financial officer said the company would stick to its annual repurchase target of $10 billion to $20 billion while prioritizing balance-sheet strength over the long term. Because energy is cyclical, our business has to perform in all cycles. We're not changing our plan around a single quarter, she said.
Chevron also announced it has realized $1.5 billion in synergies from its acquisition of Hess last year, reaching that milestone six months ahead of schedule and exceeding the original $1 billion target set at closing.
The company said its long-term cash targets have not changed regardless of margins and cash flow, and that it is positioned to be more aggressive with shareholder returns.
Total liabilities and working capital both moved in line with the guidance the company had previously given, and free cash flow covered the dividend and the buyback in full.
