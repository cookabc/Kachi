Gold dropped more than 1% on Thursday, pressured by higher oil prices that intensified inflation worries and strengthened the view that interest rates may remain elevated for an extended period.
Spot gold was down 1.1% at $4,095.75 an ounce, having earlier touched its weakest level since October 7. U.S. gold futures for December delivery fell 0.9% to $4,112.10.
A senior market analyst said further gains in oil could lift inflation expectations, making a longer stretch of higher rates more likely and creating a burden for bullion.
Crude prices climbed after additional attacks led the United States to instruct some diplomats and their family members to leave its missions in the kingdom.
Although persistent inflation, driven largely by expensive energy, has strengthened the argument for tighter monetary policy, Federal Reserve officials are expected to stay patient for the time being.
Traders assigned a 15% probability to a rate increase at the next meeting and an 87% chance to a hike in December.
Personal consumption expenditures data due later in the month, which the central bank monitors closely, may offer more guidance on the policy outlook.
The analyst said investors expect rates to remain high at both the short and long ends of the Treasury yield curve, diminishing gold's attraction by raising the cost of holding an asset that pays no yield.
Spot gold continues to aim at the recent low, as a wedge pattern indicates. Resistance lies just above, and a break above that level could open the way higher, according to a technical analyst.
Analysts' consensus estimate for the quarter had been for a modest decline, and the actual move was a clear miss against that consensus for the bulls, though a beat for anyone positioned short.
Several banks trimmed their price forecast for the year, citing a stronger dollar and the prospect that the policy rate stays higher for longer. Their revised outlook assumes the real rate stays positive through next year.
In other metals, spot silver fell 3.3%, platinum lost 2.1% and palladium declined 2.4%. Mining equities followed the move, with leverage to the metal price cutting both ways.
