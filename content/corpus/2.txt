The euro declined on Friday and was headed for a weekly loss against both the US dollar and the yen, as a selloff in French government bonds drove the country's debt risk premium to its highest since the 2011 euro-zone sovereign debt crisis.
The yen was set for a weekly gain against the dollar, supported by safe-haven demand after Thursday's selloff in AI-related stocks. The dollar index was almost unchanged over the week.
The euro came under pressure from concerns about rising unrest in France and the country's worsening budget problems. The country also faces the possibility of a rating downgrade by Moody's on Friday.
While the Macron government had threatened military assistance to quell the social unrest, more activity was likely over the weekend.
The euro was down 0.21% at $1.1191 and was on course to end the week 0.43% lower. It fell more sharply against the yen, touching 175.08 yen, its lowest since October 2025.
The dollar index, which measures the greenback against a basket including the yen and the euro, rose 0.25% to 101.82.
The yen had continued to trade at firmer levels late in the week, encouraged by more risk-off trading conditions.
Traders were looking ahead to next week's policy meetings at the Federal Reserve, the European Central Bank and the Bank of Japan. All three are expected to leave rates unchanged, but investors will seek clues about the future policy path.
After Thursday's stock market selloff, fed funds futures traders reduced bets on a December hike, though they still saw an 81% chance of such a move.
Traders priced a 66% chance of an ECB hike in December. Investors will scrutinize any signal on the rate outlook after ECB President said a moderate policy response to inflation was appropriate.
Purchasing Managers' Index data showed private-sector growth in the euro zone slowed in October to its weakest pace in four months, hurt by a renewed contraction in France.
The drop brought the PMIs back in line with other growth indicators rather than suggesting downside risks from here. France remained a drag, but the rest of the euro area was resilient.
In Japan, Friday's data showed annual core consumer inflation exceeded the central bank's 2% target in September for the first time in nine months. The reading pointed to broadening price pressures and strengthened the case for a near-term rate hike.
