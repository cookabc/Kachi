The yuan moved in a tight range after briefly reaching its strongest level in more than four years, lifted by solid export growth and a generally softer dollar.
The U.S. currency stayed subdued after dropping to a three-week low in the prior session, as lower Treasury yields and weaker oil prices diminished its appeal.
The onshore yuan touched 6.6765 per dollar in morning trading, the highest since June 2022, before paring gains slightly.
Third-quarter economic growth exceeded expectations, with surging exports offsetting cautious household spending.
Analysts said domestic economic resilience, capital inflows and rising corporate demand to convert foreign exchange earnings into yuan had combined to reinforce the upward trend. They added that the pace of appreciation could slow ahead, as geopolitical uncertainties might lend support to the dollar and slightly reduce capital inflows.
One bank estimated China drew net foreign exchange inflows of $50 billion in September, with the conversion ratio rising to 66% from 63% a month earlier.
The yuan has gained 0.4% against the dollar this month and is 4.7% stronger so far this year.
The central bank has gradually allowed the daily fixing to strengthen in line with the yuan's rise. Before the market opened it set the midpoint at its strongest since February 2023. The spot yuan may trade 2% either side of the fixed midpoint each day.
The offshore yuan stood slightly weaker. The exchange rate between the two has converged over the past year as capital controls tightened.
The dollar's index against six major currencies was little changed. A further rate cut in the United States would widen the interest differential and push the exchange rate lower still, analysts said.
The currency's appreciation matters for exporters: a stronger yuan makes Chinese goods dearer abroad, and the central bank's tolerance of it signals confidence about growth. Too fast an appreciation, though, would erode margins in exactly the sectors the export numbers depend on.
Board members have debated whether to tighten policy further. A senior official said the tightening bias would persist while inflation stays below target, though the market has priced only a modest chance of another rate cut this year.

Traders expect the yuan to appreciate further if the dollar keeps weakening, though the central bank may resist a rapid move.
