A combination of unsettled bond markets, persistent inflation and changing foreign-exchange conditions is putting earnings and capital strength at risk for Asia-focused banks as they get ready to report third-quarter results. The region's leading lenders have strong deposit bases, but analysts say higher long-end yields and macroeconomic strain are exposing weak points. Investors will focus on how much market swings have affected balance sheets, funding costs and shareholder returns.
In early October, a global bond selloff lifted the benchmark 10-year U.S. Treasury yield to a multi-year high of 5.34% before it settled near 5.11%, while Japanese yields also jumped. The effect of costlier borrowing is still flowing through regional financial institutions.
The pressure appears through three channels: liquidity, capital and profit and loss. Higher yields would hurt profits and asset values, weakening bank capital, limiting capacity to raise fresh funds and possibly leading to cuts in earnings-per-share forecasts, a measure investors follow closely.
The results will be scrutinised for signs that higher funding costs are starting to affect margins and credit quality. Analysts expect HSBC to report a 33% increase in pretax profit for July-September. DBS is forecast to show a 3.2% decline in net profit for the period.
After this month's U.S. Treasury rout, HSBC shares fell 3.8% and Standard Chartered shares dropped 9%. Pressure on bond valuations, and therefore on banks' book values, could weigh on key capital ratios and, in turn, dividend payouts. Healthy earnings can generate organic capital, however, and help offset the full effect.
Moody's warned that rising Japanese government bond yields are causing large unrealised losses for lenders with long-duration assets. The strain has already helped trigger credit downgrades at some regional banks. Further upward pressure on Japanese interest rates is likely.
In the Philippines and Indonesia, stubborn inflation and greater foreign-exchange volatility are beginning to affect asset quality and wholesale funding structures.
Regional banks are also bracing for slower wealth momentum after strong gains driven by Chinese capital inflows. Tighter Chinese scrutiny of offshore taxes could slow those flows in the near term.
